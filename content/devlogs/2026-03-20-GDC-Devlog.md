Of course I went to GDC.
I mean, I am a game developer, I am in the USA, and I can get reimbursed for my expenses.
Everybody is going, and there is no reason for me not to go.

## GDC Day 1

### Balancing TCGs with Power Sorting

I have to say, the first talk was also my favorite of the bunch. Nate Heiss started with an incredible opening, “I made 2000 cards in MTCG, none of them were banned.” Then, he introduced how he balanced the cards, with the method of power sorting. To put it briefly, you first group the cards by their functions, then sort them by their potential strength, thus being able to decide the parameters based on the order. When new cards are created, it will be easy to just put the card into the group, compare it to others and decide the parameters. He also believes that this can be used in lots of different situations, and I really agree. I’ve always been curious about how to balance cards, and in fact, I am going to balance some skills in my current semester project at ETC. This talk was so useful and literally opened my mind.

### GDC Encounters: Speed Networking Day 1

As I was waiting for the next talk, I tried to attend the speed networking event. I was disappointed. Developers had to pair up randomly and network for five minutes. I personally think that five minutes is too short, and there wasn't any useful networking happening for me.

### Game UX Mindset Masterclass: Principles and Methods

Celia Hodent came here to introduce some basics of UX. Since I had started getting interested in UI/UX, I was really looking forward to attending it. Still, the talk made me realize that game design and UX cover similar ground from two different perspectives. Some of the material had already been covered in the Game Design course I attended this semester, such as self-determination theory. But still, it is worth refreshing that knowledge.

### [The MIX + Kinda Funny](https://mediaindieexchange.com/showcases/spring-game-showcase-2026)

At the end of day 1, I went to attend The MIX + Kinda Funny, which is a huge indie game showcase. I saw a lot of different indie games and got a lot of ideas. Here are some of the games I played. One of the things that made the biggest impression on me was meeting the developers of [HYPERBEAT](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2263360/HYPERBEAT/). When I was trying their demo, I was struck by how good the UI was. The developer encouraged me to explore the art of UI, and recommended that I check out more graphic design. I really appreciate him.

## GDC Day 2

### Supporting Thousands of NPCs in 'Kingdom Come: Deliverance' & 'Kingdom Come: Deliverance II’

Originally, I thought this talk was about the design of NPCs in the game, but Petr Smrcek was mostly talking about the programming part. Still, it was a valuable lesson, and it was also really interesting to see their approach to improving performance.

### Spreadsheets Microtalks

Seeing so many developers share how they use spreadsheets was so crazy. Some are used for prototypes, while others are used for production. There are even developers who use them for localization quality assurance. Since I previously also worked on that as an intern, I was amazed by how effective this pipeline could be. It also made me want to dig deeper and learn more about spreadsheets in the future.

### 3rd Annual Game Developer's Concert

Some of the students recommended attending the concert, and it was packed with people. I was so surprised that Troy Baker was on the stage, but sadly he didn’t play anything during the concert. Generally it was a good one, and I will definitely attend next year if possible.

## GDC Day 3

### Mics in Spaces: The Importance of Field and Foley Recording

Since I pitched a project about Foley recording, I believed this was a must-attend talk for me. I first learned about Foley recording through an interview [video](https://youtu.be/WFVLWo5B81w?si=zrbCSLTdwKwVETOI) of one of the speakers, Joanna Fang. While preparing my pitch, I referenced her video several times. She also worked on the sound design for Death Stranding 2, a game I played recently, and the sound design in that game was insane. The talk itself was really awesome. I learned a lot about the actual difference between field and Foley recording, and it gave me a clearer idea of how we are going to execute the project in the future. After the talk, I had a chance to talk with Joanna, and I told her about our project. She seemed to be really interested and willing to follow up with us in the future. This was the most valuable talk for me at GDC this year.

### ALT.CTRL.GDC

I spent the whole afternoon strolling around the exhibition hall, and I was amazed by the alt.ctrl. It was crazy that there were so many weird but fun controls. In one of my favorites, you have to sell illegal games to customers. You will wear a giant coat, and when police come, you will have to hide those illegal games. Overall, it was really fun to stroll around the hall.

### ETC @ GDC: 25th Anniversary on the Road

I arrived at the party at 8, and I was able to talk with a lot of different people. I shared my Foley recording project pitch with some of the alumni. They all really liked it and gave me a lot of advice. Also, I managed to have a chat with one of the alumni who just graduated. We all discovered how fun it is to design and make a UI that breaks away from conventional design. Since we are all programmers, we have some deep knowledge and skills to enable us to make unique UIs that blend into the game. We shared our perspectives and had a wonderful time!

## GDC Day 4

### 'DEATH STRANDING 2': Making of Voxel 3D UI Map

Well, Hideo Kojima was originally going to be here, but he didn’t come. This talk was the only one related to Death Stranding 2, and I was also really curious about how they make the UI map in game. The talk focused heavily on technical art, so I could only understand about 50% of it. Still, it was impressive how they tried to make every detail in the game perfect, even though I had never noticed those details while playing.

### To Each Their Own Hell: The Journey of Writing Complex Characters in 'Limbus Company' (Presented by Project Moon)

Project Moon is a famous indie game studio in Korea, and some of the games are really famous because of the plots and characters. Watching how the writers approach the narrative is really cool. She talked about setting up a “hell” for characters, which included their own trauma and values. When these contradict each other, it can make the characters feel closer to players, and when the characters face their “hell,” it makes them more memorable.

## Summary

What an amazing experience attending GDC for the first time!

It was a lie.

I could sense the feeling of dread during the conference, and I didn’t feel really comfortable about it. GDC kind of reflects the situation of the industry, and you can sort of notice that there are too many AI talks and too many talks that are also unrelated to how to make your game better. Plus, all the layoffs just make everyone less engaged, and it was hard for me to network. It is hard to follow up with somebody else, since some of them won’t want to waste time on students. Still, seeing so many indie games being showcased during the conference, I did realize something really important.   

Take action and make games. That’s more important than just networking and doing nothing.
